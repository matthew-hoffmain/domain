> First and foremost, welcome to my website. I started this project to serve as an online portfolio, and to demonstrate 
> a few of my passioned interests in the realm between technology and law. It has now taken the additional role of a combined
> tech and legal review blog. I am a professional software engineer 
> with a paralegal certification, and I am currently applying to law schools. My background 
> lends me towards [[intellectual property]] and [[technology law]], but I am deeply interested in all aspects of legal advocacy 
> and the creation of [[informed policy]].

> In addition to showcasing some of my technical skills, I designed this website to answer questions of permissions
> in the digital age. The internet has made it easier than ever to share and distribute content, but it has also
> created confusion about what is legal and what is not. Many people believe that if something is available online,
> they can use it however they want. This is not true. Copyright law still applies to digital content, and using someone else's work
> without permission can lead to legal consequences.

> The confusion of these rights is often abused, not just by everyday users plagiarizing content, but also tech companies
> seeking to build large linguistic, image, and video databases. The concept of the [[internet-as-a-corpus]] often overlooks the nuances
> of copyright law and the importance of respecting creators' rights. [[Web-scrapers]]
> and [[data-mining]] tools can easily extract vast amounts of information from websites, but this does not mean that
> all of that information is free to use. Additionally, AI models trained on copyrighted material without permission
> raise significant ethical and legal questions, such as whether their resulting outputs are considered 
> [[copyright derived works]]. **Currently, there exists no legal standard to ensure that copyrighted material is prevented from being
> scraped or used in AI training datasets.\***

> \**There is a technical standard called [[robots.txt]] that websites can use to indicate which parts of their site 
> should not be accessed by web crawlers. However, this standard is not legally binding, and there is no guarantee that 
> web scrapers will comply with it. It is entirely voluntarily and largely ignored by nefarious actors.*

> The legal model of copyright is based on existing standards that were created long before the internet existed.
> These standards were designed to protect creators' rights and incentivize the creation of new works. However, they 
> often fail to account for the unique challenges and opportunities presented by digital content.
> As a result, there is a growing need for updated copyright laws that reflect the realities of the digital age. 

> Overall, the purpose of the website's design is to depict a counter-standard to platforms with stealable content.
> **This website is built to serve as an exemplary domain comprised solely of content that is freely redistributable, 
> from art and music to [[open-source]] software.** You can borrow, modify, and alter anything you find here, 
> but you can't steal anything because it already belongs to you. The only caveat is that you must comply with the terms of the
> respective licenses, such as providing attribution where required.

> There are plenty of websites with similar design philosophies, such as [[Wikipedia]], [[Creative Commons]], and [[Open Source Initiative]].
> The website [Classicals.de](https://www.classicals.de/) specifically served as a major inspiration for this project.
> The difference between this site and others is that the source-code for this website is also openly available,
> and uses [[copyleft]] software licensing via the [[MIT License]], which provides permissibility similar to Creative Commons.
> While this form of software licensing is common in the [[open-source]] community, it is rarely applied to entire websites.

> If you are interested in finding which software licensing is best for your project, I highly suggest visiting the
> [Choose a License](https://choosealicense.com/) website, which provides a comprehensive overview of popular [[open-source]] licenses and
> their respective terms.

>  This website aims to demonstrate that it is possible to create a digital space where all content is freely available and legally safe to use,
> in hopes that it inspires the conception and emergence of a contrasting standard for websites to protect their content from theft.
